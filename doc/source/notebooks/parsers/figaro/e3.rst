FIGARO E3
=========

.. container:: mario-companion

   :download:`Download notebook <e3.ipynb>`

Download the CSV files and classifications from the
`JRC FIGARO-E3 collection <https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/collection/id-00403>`_.
Keep the extracted files under one root; distribution subfolders are allowed.
Import the complete supply-use system:

.. code-block:: python

   from pathlib import Path
   import mario

   root = Path("/path/to/FIGARO E3")
   db = mario.parse_figaro_e3(
       path=root, table="SUT", year=2015, employment="both", calc_all=False,
   )

The published system has 46 regions, including Rest of the World, 176
activities and 213 commodities. ``S`` is transposed from the source supply
table; ``U`` and ``Yc`` retain commodity origins and industry/final-demand
destinations. All 11 primary-input and adjustment rows are retained, including
their direct final-demand values in ``VY``. Native codes remain the model
labels; ``db.info["figaro_e3"]["classifications"]`` supplies their descriptions.

Choose files and formats
------------------------

The same economic data is published as a wide ``matrix_*.csv`` or a long
``flatfile_*.csv``. Both representations are valid. ``input_format="auto"``
prefers economic matrices and uses an available flat file where needed.
Select ``"matrix"`` or ``"flatfile"`` to require that format for all inputs.
Flat files are read in chunks; the full table is retained in the model.

The classification workbook is required for every parse. Choose the remaining
files according to the requested system:

.. list-table:: Economic inputs
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 22 78

   * - System
     - Filename stems, after ``matrix_`` or ``flatfile_``
   * - SUT
     - ``FIGARO-e-ic-supply_millionEUR_2015.csv`` and
       ``FIGARO-e-ic-use_millionEUR_2015.csv``
   * - Industry IOT
     - ``FIGARO-e-ic-io-D_ind-by-ind_millionEUR_2015.csv``
   * - Product IOT
     - ``FIGARO-e-ic-io-B_prod-by-prod_millionEUR_2015.csv``

Model D is industry-by-industry; model B is product-by-product. They are
different economic tables, rather than duplicate downloads:

.. code-block:: python

   industry = mario.parse_figaro_e3(
       path=root, table="IOT", iot_mode="industry", year=2015,
   )
   product = mario.parse_figaro_e3(
       path=root, table="IOT", iot_mode="product", year=2015,
       extensions=False,
   )

The shared entry point also accepts
``mario.parse_figaro(path=root, variant="E3", table="SUT")``. Standard
FIGARO retains its Eurostat API workflow. E3's default IOT mode is industry,
matching the native extension axes. E3 keeps its own classification and does
not inherit the standard 64-industry FIGARO concordances.

Browser suffixes such as ``copy``, ``-2`` and ``(1)`` are accepted when the
candidate files are byte-identical. Conflicting copies or classification
revisions raise an error: put the chosen release in a separate directory.
The parser does not infer a preferred revision from download timestamps.
The GAMS construction-code archive is not required for importing the CSVs.

Select extensions
-----------------

``extensions="all"`` reads emissions, energy and employment. Select individual
groups with a list, or pass ``extensions=False`` for economic flows alone.
The groups have different alternatives and accounting scopes:

* ``emissions="co2eq"`` imports the published four-gas AR5/GWP100 CO2eq
  account, from ``AEM_Gg_CO2e`` files. ``emissions="mass"`` selects
  ``AEM_Gg``: CO2, CH4 and N2O are in kt of each gas, while F-gases remain
  in kt CO2eq. Import one inventory at a time. A Gg is numerically one kt.
* Energy includes PES, NEU, FEU, EOU, LOSS and NENE in TJ. Auto uses the
  complete ``flatfile_FIGARO-e_ENE_TJ_2015.csv`` when available; matrix-only
  parsing requires all six indicator files. Energy indicators overlap:
  FEU, EOU and LOSS are components of NEU, so summing the rows is not an
  energy footprint.
* ``employment="EDU"`` (default) and ``"OCC"`` are alternative estimates
  based on education and occupation. ``"both"`` retains them as separately
  labelled six-row inventories. Do not add the two methods together.
  Employment is in thousand persons and has no direct final-demand extension.

Direct final-demand emissions and energy are aligned by their codes, including
the published extension files' different final-demand ordering and trailing
spaces. Source signs are preserved, including negative energy/adjustment flows.

For the already characterised CO2eq account:

.. code-block:: python

   db.calc_ghg(profile="figaro_e3", label="GHG source AR5 GWP100")

This sums the four gas rows, including direct final demand, once. Changing an
IPCC report or time-horizon argument does not recharacterise those source
CO2eq values. The gas-mass option retains mixed native units and therefore
requires an explicit characterization recipe and output unit.

Product IOT extensions
----------------------

The published extensions use industries, not products. To allocate them to
the model B IOT, explicitly set
``extension_allocation="industry_technology"`` and supply the complete supply
table. MARIO divides each industry's extension by its output, then allocates
flows to products through that industry's supply. This is a derived extension
under the industry technology assumption, rather than a published product
account. Direct final-demand extensions retain their source values.

Allocation fails if an industry has nonzero extensions but zero supply output;
the parser does not discard those flows. Use the SUT or industry IOT, or read
the product IOT with ``extensions=False`` in that case.
The published 2015 bundle triggers this guard with all extension groups
selected. The native SUT and industry IOT retain those accounts unchanged.

Source and calculation scope
----------------------------

The source year is 2015. Dates in the classification and metadata filenames
refer to supporting-document revisions, not observation years. The files use
CC BY 4.0; credit the JRC and the source authors and indicate changes. See the
`source methods paper <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04431-z>`_ for the
construction and inventory boundaries.

The parser reads local files and keeps the complete inter-country scope.
Use ``calc_all=False`` for an initial import. Monetary flows are in million
EUR; use sufficient RAM for full dense economic blocks. A solve-based session
preset prefers optional UMFPACK and falls back to SciPy.

Full interfaces: :func:`~mario.parse_figaro_e3` and :func:`~mario.parse_figaro`.
